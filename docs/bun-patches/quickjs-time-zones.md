## QuickJS time zones

Class **Host feature**. QuickJS-ng 0.16.2 (bundled by rquickjs-sys 0.13.0) computes local time
in `getTimezoneOffset` (`third_party/rquickjs-sys/quickjs/quickjs.c`) with `localtime_r`, and
wasi-libc's knows only UTC, so `Date` ignored `process.env.TZ` on QuickJS. Now local time
follows `TZ` as on JSC ([`process.env` and `TZ`](process-env-semantics.md)): `getHours`,
`getTimezoneOffset`, `toString`, `toTimeString`, `toLocaleString` and the fallback `Intl`.
QuickJS reads the offset on every call, so a `Date` made before a write follows the new zone.

### Zone data

The zones come from data the runtime already ships: the FormatJS `Intl` fallback's
`add-all-tz` payload (every IANA zone with its transitions from the earliest record through
2099) and its link table. `scripts/vendor-intl.ts` now writes them to their own module,
`crates/bunwasm-runtime/src/js/tz_data.js` (1.36 MB raw), and `intl.js` reads that module
instead of carrying them inline, so the guest carries no second copy and QuickJS can read a
zone without loading the 1.29 MB `Intl` bundle. Regenerating from the pinned lockfile gives the
same `intl.js` bytes plus the split.

`crates/bunwasm-runtime/src/js/time_zone.ts` (original) resolves a name as ICU does for
JavaScriptCore: exact case (Bun keeps the previous zone for `asia/tokyo`), FormatJS's links
(`US/Pacific` is `America/Los_Angeles`), `GMT`, `Etc/GMT` and `Etc/UTC` as `UTC`, and
`EST5EDT`, `CST6CDT`, `MST7MDT` and `PST8PDT` as their cities (tzdata 2024b links, which
FormatJS's data predates). It turns the zone's entries into `[offset, time, offset, ...]`
(seconds) and calls the `setLocalTimeOffsets` native. An unknown name changes nothing and
answers null, an empty name restores UTC, and the UTC names never load the data. The first
other zone costs about 23 ms on QuickJS (loading `tz_data.js`), later ones about 1 ms. Checked
against ICU (native Bun) every three days at four times a day from 1950 to 2060: no offset
differs for New York, London, Lord Howe, Kolkata, `Etc/GMT+3`, the UTC names or `EST5EDT`.

### Engine

- `quickjs.c` (patch): `JS_bunwasm_SetLocalTimeOffsets` copies the table into fixed storage
  (1024 transitions; the largest zone, Europe/Guernsey, has 365), and `getTimezoneOffset`
  answers from it with a binary search while one is set. Building a date from local fields
  (`set_date_fields`) uses `getLocalTimezoneOffset`: of the offsets a day before and a day
  after, the first that maps back to the same local time, so a time in a spring-forward gap
  moves past it and a repeated fall-back time is its first occurrence, as JavaScriptCore does.
  Without a table, the libc's path runs unchanged.
- `Engine::set_local_time_offsets` (default false; QuickJS's `vm.rs` calls the hook) and the
  `setLocalTimeOffsets` native in `value_natives.rs` carry the table from JavaScript.
- `bootstrap.js` (QuickJS only): `toString` and `toTimeString` append the zone's long name, as
  JavaScriptCore does ("GMT-0500 (Eastern Standard Time)", "(Coordinated Universal Time)" for
  UTC), from the fallback `Intl`, corrected by `zone_names.js`; `toLocaleString` formats through
  the fallback `Intl` with ECMA-402's date and time defaults.
- `crates/bunwasm-runtime/src/js/zone_names.js` is generated by
  `scripts/generate-zone-names.ts` with native Bun 1.4.4 (ICU 78.3): the 86 standard and
  daylight names of 2024 where ICU's English name differs from FormatJS's ("India Standard
  Time" rather than "GMT+05:30", "British Summer Time" rather than "GMT+01:00"). 4.3 KB raw.

### Size

The QuickJS guest grows by 16.3 KB raw and 2.9 KB gzip: `bun.wasm` 21,524,011 to 21,535,662
bytes (gzip 6,774,998 to 6,775,992) and `quickjs.wasm` 1,460,842 to 1,465,482 bytes (gzip
476,125 to 478,075).

### Differences from native Bun 1.4.4

- The Browser engine keeps its host's zone: a page cannot change it (host limit).
- `toString`'s names are 2024's; a past era can name a zone differently (London in 1970 prints
  "British Summer Time" where ICU prints "Greenwich Mean Time").
- After 2099 a zone keeps its last offset (the data's last transition is in 2099); ICU goes on
  applying the daylight saving rules.
- Offsets are whole minutes, as QuickJS's `getTimezoneOffset` returns them, so a local mean time
  before the zones (New York before 1883 is UTC-4:56:02) loses its seconds.
- Native Bun's argument-less `toLocaleString()` keeps the zone it first formatted in; bun-wasm's
  follows `TZ`, as its `toLocaleString("en-US")` and `Intl` do in Bun.
- The fallback `Intl` (FormatJS, on QuickJS and JSC) puts U+202F before AM/PM where Bun prints a
  space; that is not about zones.

### Tests

`process-env-tz-zones` (QuickJS and JSC) compares zone by zone with native Bun 1.4.4 run in UTC:
New York, London, Lord Howe's 30-minute shift and its transition instants, Kolkata, `Etc/GMT+3`,
GMT, Etc/UTC, EST5EDT, `US/Pacific`, case, a gap and an overlap in New York, unknown zones, an
empty value and delete. `process-env-tz` (worker rules and Intl) now runs on QuickJS too.
